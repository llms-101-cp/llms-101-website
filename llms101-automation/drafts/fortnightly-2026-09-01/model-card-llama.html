Llama is Meta's family of open-weight AI models, including Llama 4 Scout and Maverick alongside the earlier Llama 3.x line, which can be downloaded for self-hosting, fine-tuned, and deployed on AWS Bedrock and other enterprise cloud platforms under Meta's custom license. As of late 2026, however, Meta has shifted its frontier work to a new lineup: Meta released Muse Spark, which it calls "the first step on our scaling ladder" as the team builds a family of Muse models. That pivot has real consequences for Llama — Meta's official statement says the "Llama line will continue separately," but the consensus is that Llama is in maintenance mode, with all significant resources flowing to Meta Superintelligence Labs and Muse Spark.

Meta has not fully abandoned open weights, though. After a hosted, not open-weight Muse Spark launched in April 2026, the company followed with a 131,072-token open release: Meta shipped Muse Glimmer-30B on Hugging Face on August 10, 2026 — a 30-billion-parameter dense, multimodal, agent-tuned model under a plain Apache 2.0 license, with no Llama-style monthly-active-user cap. Meta is now running two release tracks in parallel: closed, hosted frontier models (Muse Spark 1.1 via the Meta Model API) and open-weight distillations sized for local hardware (Muse Glimmer, and soon Muse Spark 1.2). For most beginners, Llama remains a solid free option for private, self-hosted chat and text tasks — just know it's no longer Meta's leading edge.
